# Acceptable Use Policy

Huntagent is an offensive-security agent that can run real tools — shell
commands, network scanners, web requests, and more — against live systems. With
that capability comes a hard line on how it may be used. **By downloading,
building, or running Huntagent, you agree to this policy.**

## You may use Huntagent to test

- **Systems you own** outright.
- **Systems you have explicit, written authorization to test** — for example a
  signed penetration-testing agreement, a scoped engagement, or a bug-bounty
  program whose rules expressly permit your activity and stay within its scope.
- **Deliberately vulnerable practice targets** you control or that are published
  for training (local labs, CTF challenges, intentionally vulnerable VMs, and
  ranges that permit it).
- **Defensive, research, and educational work** on the above — detection
  engineering, forensics, hardening, and learning.

## You may not use Huntagent to

- Access, scan, probe, disrupt, or test **any system you do not own and are not
  explicitly authorized to test**.
- Exceed the **scope, targets, or time window** of an authorization you do hold.
- Conduct any activity that is **illegal** in your jurisdiction or the target's,
  or that breaches a contract, terms of service, or program policy you are bound
  by.
- Cause **harm, data loss, service disruption, or extortion**, or attempt to
  evade detection in furtherance of unauthorized activity.
- Develop, stage, or deliver **malware, ransomware, or botnet/C2 infrastructure**
  for use against systems you are not authorized to attack.

## Your responsibilities

- **Get authorization first.** Obtaining valid, written permission for every
  target is your responsibility, before you point Huntagent at anything.
- **Know the law.** Unauthorized access to computers and networks is a crime in
  most jurisdictions (e.g. the U.S. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, the UK
  Computer Misuse Act, and equivalents elsewhere). Compliance is entirely yours.
- **Review what the agent does.** Huntagent is not a sandbox — see
  [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md). It asks before running commands so *you* can
  decide; approving an action means you take responsibility for it.
- **Stay in scope.** Keep every action within the targets and limits of your
  authorization.
